I started research as an undergraduate assistant at the University of Florida, working under Prof. Siyao Xu on computational plasma physics. This project lasted from October 2025 until May 2025.

Project Title: Numerical Measurement of Mean Free Path of High-Energy Weakly-Collisional Plasmas

Weakly collisional plasmas posess collision dynamics far different than ideal gasses, making the mean free path of particles difficult to define. We used numerical simulations on the University of Florida's HiPerGator supercomputing cluster to track an individual particle's time evolution, and tracked particle collisions using Python codes. The key is to define particle collisions as a 90-degree turn in space, allowing us to perform a numerical measurement of the mean free path.

This project also resulted in a presentation at the UF Physics Spring Undergraduate Poster session. This project was an excellent introduction to physics research, and I became comfortable with Python and high-performance computing clusters.
